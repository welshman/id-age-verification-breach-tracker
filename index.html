Why this exists

Passports, driver's licences, and national ID cards are increasingly uploaded to third-party services for “Know Your Customer” (KYC) checks, age verification on social media and adult sites, and onboarding for crypto exchanges, gig platforms, and fintechs. Unlike a leaked password, you cannot reset your date of birth or passport number. When these services are breached, the consequences last far longer — yet public awareness of ID-verification breaches lags far behind awareness of email/password breaches.

This site tracks publicly reported breaches of identity-verification and age-verification services and documents, so that anyone can quickly check which companies have had confirmed incidents, what kind of data was exposed, and what to do about it.

How it works

1. Public research

We track breaches reported by journalists, security researchers, and companies themselves — never by attempting to access breach data directly.

2. Automated updates

A scheduled GitHub Actions workflow scans public feeds and keyword-matches new candidate breaches, opening them for review rather than auto-publishing unverified claims.

3. Static & private

The whole site is static files on GitHub Pages, with no backend server and no database.
